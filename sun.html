Anatomy of the Sun

Solar Phenomena

Solar Flares

Sudden bursts of radiation released when magnetic energy near sunspots snaps and reconnects, flooding space with X-rays and UV light in minutes.

Sunspots

Darker, cooler patches on the photosphere where strong magnetic fields suppress the flow of heat from below — some grow larger than Earth.

Coronal Mass Ejections

Huge eruptions of plasma and magnetic field hurled from the corona — when aimed at Earth, they can trigger geomagnetic storms and auroras.

Solar Wind

A constant stream of charged particles flowing outward from the corona, shaping every planet's magnetosphere as it sweeps through the Solar System.

Prominences

Huge loops of glowing plasma arcing above the surface, held aloft by magnetic fields for days or weeks before collapsing back down.

Auroras

When solar wind and CMEs strike Earth's magnetic field, charged particles collide with the atmosphere and glow — the northern and southern lights.

How the Sun Makes Heat & Light

In short: nuclear fusion in the core, then a very long trip outward.

  1. Step 1

    Fusion in the core

    Immense pressure and ~15M°C heat fuse hydrogen nuclei into helium, releasing energy as gamma rays.

  2. Step 2

    Radiative zone crawl

    Photons bounce between particles so often it can take tens of thousands of years to drift outward.

  3. Step 3

    Convective churn

    Closer to the surface, rising and sinking plasma currents carry the remaining energy upward much faster.

  4. Step 4

    Light escapes

    At the photosphere, energy finally radiates freely into space as the sunlight and heat we receive on Earth.
