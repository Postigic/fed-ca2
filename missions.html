Mission Timeline

  1. 1957

    First artificial satellite.

    Launch Date:
    4 October 1957
    Country:
    Soviet Union
    Rocket:
    Sputnik 8K71PS
    Orbital Period:
    96 minutes

    Sputnik 1's simple radio beeps, audible to amateur radio operators worldwide, triggered the Space Race and prompted the United States to found NASA less than a year later.

  2. 1961

    First human spaceflight.

    Launch Date:
    12 April 1961
    Country:
    Soviet Union
    Cosmonaut:
    Yuri Gagarin
    Duration:
    108 minutes

    Gagarin's single orbit of Earth proved humans could survive spaceflight and made him instantly famous worldwide. The flight's success intensified the Space Race, pushing the United States toward its own crewed program and, eventually, the Moon.

  3. 1969

    First crewed Moon landing.

    Launch Date:
    16 July 1969
    Landing:
    20 July 1969
    Crew:
    Neil Armstrong, Buzz Aldrin, Michael Collins
    Spacecraft:
    Columbia (CM), Eagle (LM)

    An estimated 650 million people watched Armstrong's first steps live, one of the most-viewed broadcasts in history, fulfilling President Kennedy's 1961 pledge to land a man on the Moon before the decade's end.

  4. 1977

    Grand Tour probe, now in interstellar space.

    Launch Date:
    5 September 1977
    Country:
    United States
    Flybys:
    Jupiter, Saturn
    Status:
    Interstellar space since 2012

    Voyager 1 carries the Golden Record, a phonograph disc of sounds and images meant to represent life on Earth to any intelligent life that might find it. Nearly five decades after launch, it's still transmitting data from farther away than any other human-made object has ever traveled.

  5. 1990

    Orbiting observatory that reshaped modern astronomy.

    Launch Date:
    24 April 1990
    Partners:
    NASA, ESA
    Orbit Altitude:
    ~535 km
    Servicing Missions:
    5

    A flawed mirror nearly doomed the mission at launch, but a 1993 spacewalk repair turned Hubble into one of the most productive scientific instruments ever built, capturing images like the Hubble Deep Field that reshaped how far, and how old, the observable universe was known to be.

  6. 1998

    Largest space laboratory, continuously crewed since 2000.

    First Module:
    Zarya, 20 November 1998
    Partners:
    NASA, Roscosmos, ESA, JAXA, CSA
    Continuous Occupation:
    Since 2 Nov 2000
    Orbital Speed:
    ~28,000 km/h

    Assembled from more than 40 modules launched over more than a decade, the ISS orbits Earth roughly every 90 minutes and has hosted over 280 people from more than 20 countries, making it one of the longest-running examples of international cooperation in history.

  7. 2015

    First landing of an orbital-class rocket booster.

    Landing Date:
    21 December 2015
    Company:
    SpaceX
    Landing Site:
    Landing Zone 1, Cape Canaveral
    Mission:
    Orbcomm-2

    Every orbital rocket before this flight had been thrown away after a single use. Landing the booster upright and intact opened the door to routine reuse, which has cut the cost of reaching orbit more than any advance since the Space Shuttle.

  8. 2020

    First crewed flight on a commercially built spacecraft.

    Launch Date:
    30 May 2020
    Operator:
    SpaceX, for NASA
    Crew:
    Robert Behnken, Douglas Hurley
    Destination:
    International Space Station

    Demo-2 ended a nine-year gap in US crewed launches since the Space Shuttle's retirement, and was the first time NASA astronauts reached orbit aboard a spacecraft built and flown by a private company.

  9. 2022

    Hubble's successor, built to see the universe's first galaxies.

    Launch Date:
    25 December 2021
    First Images:
    12 July 2022
    Partners:
    NASA, ESA, CSA
    Orbit:
    Sun–Earth L2, ~1.5 million km out

    Webb's infrared instruments see through cosmic dust to light from galaxies formed within a few hundred million years of the Big Bang, further back in time than Hubble could reach.

  10. 2022

    Uncrewed test flight paving the way back to the Moon.

    Launch:
    16 November 2022
    Spacecraft:
    Orion
    Mission Duration:
    25.5 days
    Max Distance:
    ~432,000 km from Earth

    Though it carried no crew, Artemis I proved out the Space Launch System rocket and Orion's heat shield ahead of Artemis II and III, the first attempts to return astronauts to the lunar surface since Apollo 17 in 1972.
